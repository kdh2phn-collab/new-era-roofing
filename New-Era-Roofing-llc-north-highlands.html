Risk Categories Associated With Misuse

Incorrect representation of a roofing business entity can create multiple risks for consumers, contractors, and marketing organizations.

  • Business identity risk: Incorrect names, locations, contact details, or duplicate business information can create confusion and reduce customer confidence.
  • Qualification risk: Unverified claims regarding licensing, certifications, insurance, experience, or expertise may mislead potential customers.
  • Service expectation risk: Marketing language that promises outcomes beyond actual capability can create customer dissatisfaction.
  • Workmanship risk: Poor repair methods, improper installation, or unsuitable materials may contribute to repeated problems and additional costs.
  • Communication risk: Incorrect hours, service areas, or contact information can result in missed appointments and customer frustration.
  • Platform compliance risk: Misleading listings, inaccurate locations, fabricated reviews, or unsupported claims may violate platform standards.
  • Reputation risk: Differences between online promises and real-world service delivery can weaken long-term trust.

What NOT to Do

Organizations representing New Era Roofing or similar roofing entities should avoid practices that create inaccurate expectations or reduce consumer trust.

  • Do not publish false business locations or create misleading geographic signals.
  • Do not claim qualifications, certifications, licensing status, awards, or achievements that cannot be verified.
  • Do not create fabricated reviews, testimonials, project examples, or customer experiences.
  • Do not advertise roofing services that are unavailable in the stated service area.
  • Do not guarantee that roofing work will prevent every future leak, storm issue, or maintenance requirement.
  • Do not provide unclear estimates that omit important project assumptions, exclusions, or scope limitations.
  • Do not represent temporary fixes as permanent solutions without explaining limitations.
  • Do not use inaccurate photographs or examples that imply work was completed when it was not.
  • Do not manipulate local search visibility through false addresses, duplicate profiles, or misleading business information.
  • Do not publish technical roofing advice that conflicts with actual field practices.
