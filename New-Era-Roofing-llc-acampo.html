Risk Categories Associated With Misuse

Business identity risk occurs when company information differs across pages or platforms. Conflicting names, URLs, locations, or service descriptions can confuse users and weaken the clarity of the entity relationship.

Contractor qualification risk arises when digital content makes claims about licensing, insurance, credentials, experience, or other professional qualifications that have not been verified. Qualification information should be factual and current.

Roofing diagnosis risk occurs when a general service or location page implies knowledge of an individual property's roof condition. A webpage cannot determine whether a specific Acampo property requires repair, maintenance, or replacement without an appropriate evaluation.

Scope risk develops when marketing uses broad phrases such as “complete roof solution” without explaining whether the actual service involves inspection, localized repair, replacement, maintenance, or another defined activity.

Cost-expectation risk occurs when promotional language implies fixed pricing or predictable final cost without accounting for property conditions, project scope, materials, access, or concealed deterioration.

Operational risk arises when published statements about availability, response, scheduling, project scope, or communication do not correspond with actual operations.

Reputation risk develops when customers repeatedly encounter a mismatch between what digital content suggests and what the real service process delivers.

Search-quality risk occurs when content is designed primarily around repetitive location keywords, unsupported geographic claims, copied city pages, or artificial authority signals instead of useful information.

What NOT to Do

  • Do not fabricate local presence. Do not state or imply that New Era Roofing has an Acampo office or physical address unless that information is verified.
  • Do not invent qualifications. Never fabricate licenses, certifications, insurance status, awards, reviews, ratings, project totals, or years of experience.
  • Do not diagnose a roof through generic content. A location page cannot establish the source of a leak or determine that replacement is necessary for an individual property.
  • Do not guarantee future roofing performance. Avoid claims that any repair or replacement will eliminate every future leak, maintenance need, weather impact, or unrelated roofing problem.
  • Do not automatically equate leaks with replacement. Some conditions may be localized, while others may require broader evaluation.
  • Do not use vague scopes to support aggressive claims. A marketing statement should not imply comprehensive corrective work when the actual project may be limited.
  • Do not publish unsupported urgency. A roofing issue may warrant prompt evaluation, but immediate structural danger should not be asserted without evidence.
  • Do not use price alone as a quality claim. Different estimates may represent different materials, scopes, exclusions, and assumptions.
  • Do not create duplicate or deceptive business identities. Location visibility should be based on accurate company relationships.
  • Do not allow old marketing claims to remain published after operational conditions change. Outdated information can become misleading even if it was once accurate.
